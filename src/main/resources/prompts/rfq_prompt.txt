========================================================
CRITICAL RULE #1 — PURCHASE QUANTITY
========================================================
"quantity" means the NUMBER OF UNITS/ITEMS THE BUYER WANTS TO PURCHASE.
Examples of valid purchase quantity:
"Quantity: 4 Units" → quantity = 4
"Quantity: 3,000 Meters" → quantity = 3000
"Quantity: 20 Units" → quantity = 20
"Quantity: seven units" → quantity = 7
"Quantity: ten pieces" → quantity = 10
"Quantity: two thousand units" → quantity = 2000

========================================================
Numbers appearing inside product specifications MUST NOT be treated as purchase quantity unless the email explicitly identifies that number as the quantity to purchase.
Examples of SPECIFICATIONS (NOT QUANTITY):

========================================================
CRITICAL RULE #4 — NUMBER WORDS
========================================================
Convert English number words into numeric values ONLY when they represent the PURCHASE QUANTITY.
Examples:
"seven units" → 7
"ten pieces" → 10
"twenty laptops" → 20

========================================================
CRITICAL RULE #5 — CONTEXT-BASED QUANTITY EXTRACTION
========================================================
A number is a purchase quantity ONLY when it is associated with purchasing/order language such as:

Example:
"We require 5 Industrial Water Treatment Systems." → quantity = 5
"Please quote for 5 systems." → quantity = 5
"Quantity: 5 systems." → quantity = 5

But:
"Industrial Water Treatment System with 10,000 LPH capacity." → quantity = null

CRITICAL RULE #6 — ITEM-LEVEL BRAND, SPECIFICATION, LOCATION & DATE ISOLATION
========================================================
Extract itemDescription, brand, specification, partCode, quantity, uom, deliveryLocation, and deliveryDate independently for EACH item listed in the "items" array.
